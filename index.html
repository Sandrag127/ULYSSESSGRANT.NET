THE CAMPAIGN OF 1868

A Nation Seeking Peace

The presidential election of 1868 was the first presidential election held after the American Civil War. The country was confronting the difficult questions of Reconstruction, civil rights, national debt, taxation, and the future of the Union.

General Ulysses S. Grant became the Republican nominee, with Schuyler Colfax selected as his running mate. The campaign emphasized Reconstruction, protection of civil and political rights, government economy, and restoration of peace.

“LET US HAVE PEACE.” — Ulysses S. Grant, 1868

CANDIDATE BIOGRAPHY

Ulysses S. Grant

U.S. GRANT 1822 — 1885

Historical-style campaign portrait placeholder.

Ulysses S. Grant was born on April 27, 1822, at Point Pleasant, Ohio. His family soon moved to Georgetown, Ohio, where his father, Jesse Root Grant, operated a tannery. Grant attended the United States Military Academy at West Point and graduated in 1843. He served in the Mexican-American War before leaving active army service in 1854.

After several difficult years as a farmer and businessman, Grant moved with his family to Galena, Illinois, where he worked in his father's leather business. The outbreak of the Civil War in 1861 brought him back into military service. Grant quickly rose through the Union ranks after victories at Fort Henry and Fort Donelson. His victory at Fort Donelson earned him the famous nickname “Unconditional Surrender” Grant.

Grant's military career continued through the major campaigns of the Civil War. President Abraham Lincoln appointed him Lieutenant General and placed him in command of the Union armies in 1864. Grant coordinated Union military operations across multiple theaters and pursued the Confederate Army of Northern Virginia under General Robert E. Lee.

On April 9, 1865, Lee surrendered to Grant at Appomattox Court House in Virginia. The surrender became one of the defining moments of the Civil War and helped bring the conflict to an end. Grant's reputation as the Union's leading general made him one of the most recognizable figures in the United States.

Following the war, Grant became increasingly associated with the Republican position on Reconstruction. He supported congressional efforts to protect the rights of African Americans and supported the continuation of Reconstruction in the former Confederate states.

In May 1868, the Republican National Convention in Chicago unanimously selected Grant as its presidential nominee. Schuyler Colfax, Speaker of the House of Representatives, was chosen as his running mate. Grant accepted the nomination while emphasizing a short, memorable message: “Let Us Have Peace.”

At forty-six years old, Grant entered the 1868 election as a celebrated military commander but a relatively inexperienced national political candidate. Unlike the modern style of presidential campaigning, Grant generally remained at his home in Galena while Republican organizers, speakers, newspapers, clubs, and veterans campaigned on behalf of the ticket.

CANDIDATE ISSUE POSITIONS

Republican Principles of 1868

The following section summarizes the Republican platform that Grant accepted as the party's presidential nominee.

01

Reconstruction

The Republican platform supported the Reconstruction policy of Congress. It argued that the federal government should sustain the new governments and constitutions established in the former Confederate states and prevent those states from falling back into disorder or government by military force.

02

Civil & Political Rights

The Republican platform supported equal civil and political rights and maintained that congressional guarantees of suffrage for loyal men in the South should be preserved. The platform connected political rights with Reconstruction and the protection of loyal citizens.

03

National Debt

Republicans argued that the United States should honor its national debt in good faith. The platform rejected repudiation and supported managing the debt over a fair period while seeking lower interest rates when this could be accomplished honestly.

04

Taxation

The platform called for taxation to be equalized and reduced as rapidly as the nation's financial obligations permitted. Republicans connected responsible taxation with preserving the government's credit and meeting obligations created during the Civil War.

05

Government Reform

The Republican platform called for the federal government to be administered with strict economy. It also demanded reform in response to what Republicans regarded as corruption and misuse of government power during Andrew Johnson's presidency.

06

Peace & Union

Grant's campaign presented peace as a central theme after four years of civil war. His acceptance message concluded with the words “Let Us Have Peace,” a phrase that became the campaign's best-known slogan.

Historical note: These positions represent the Republican Party platform of 1868 and the historical context surrounding Grant's campaign. They should not be interpreted as positions on modern political questions.
